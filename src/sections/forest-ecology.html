Food webs, energy and nutrients

A few dominant species, a slow nutrient cycle, enormous carbon stores and fire as the great recycler.

Food web and energy flow

Energy enters the forest when conifers, shrubs, mosses and lichens capture sunlight. It then passes up a chain of consumers. At each step most of the energy is used for living (movement, growth and keeping warm) and lost as heat, so there are far fewer predators than prey and far more biomass in plants than in animals. Nutrient cycling: slow decay and fire

Cold temperatures slow decomposition, so nutrients stay locked in needles, wood and moss for years.{{cite:saupe_conifers}} Fungi associated with tree roots are central to this cycle: in one Swedish study, roots and their fungi supplied 50–70% of the carbon stored in boreal forest soil.{{cite:clemmensen2013}} Fire acts as a fast-forward button, releasing nutrients from burned plant material back into the soil; the U.S. Forest Service lists nutrient recycling among the benefits of the “right fire at the right place at the right time.”{{cite:usfs_rxfire}}

Key species and relationships

  • Predator and prey: the snowshoe hare–Canada lynx cycle is one of the best-known population cycles in ecology.{{cite:krebs2001}}
  • Seed predator and tree: red squirrels hoard white spruce cones, and mast seeding by the spruce periodically overwhelms them.{{cite:fletcher2010}}
  • Mutualism: conifer roots partner with fungi (mycorrhizae) that trade soil nutrients for the tree’s sugars; in boreal forests these root–fungus partnerships are also major builders of soil carbon.{{cite:clemmensen2013}}
  • Dependence on other species: boreal owls nest in holes excavated by woodpeckers.{{cite:cornell_borealowl}}
  • Disturbance-dependent species: black spruce and lodgepole pine regenerate mainly after fire.{{cite:feis_picmar,feis_pinconl}}

Biodiversity and productivity

Compared with tropical or temperate forests, the boreal forest has relatively few tree species, and the dense canopy limits the understory.{{cite:ucmp_forests}} Its animals include woodpeckers, hawks, moose, bears, weasels, lynx, foxes, wolves, deer, hares, chipmunks, shrews and bats.{{cite:ucmp_forests}} Productivity is low on cold, wet soils (black spruce stands are the least productive boreal forest type) but the biome is so large that its total contribution to Earth’s carbon cycle is enormous.{{cite:feis_picmar,clemmensen2013}}

Ecosystem services: why it matters to people

Canada’s government describes its boreal forest as storing carbon, purifying air and water and regulating the climate, and notes that because so much of the world’s boreal zone lies in Canada, its forest affects environmental health worldwide.{{cite:nrcan_boreal}} The forest supplies timber and other products, minerals, energy and hydroelectric power, and supports jobs and tourism. It is also home to many Indigenous communities: 70% of Canada’s Aboriginal communities are in forested regions.{{cite:nrcan_boreal}}

  • Climate regulation: long-term carbon storage in trees and soils
  • Clean water: forests filter water flowing to rivers and lakes
  • Resources: lumber, pulp and paper, minerals, energy
  • Culture: traditional lands and food sources for Indigenous peoples
  • Wildlife habitat: moose, caribou, bears, wolves and many birds
