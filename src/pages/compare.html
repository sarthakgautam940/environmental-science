Two ecosystems, side by side

One is a land biome shaped by cold, snow and fire; the other is a water life zone shaped by salt, tides and mud. Laid next to each other, they turn out to solve surprisingly similar problems.

Same challenge, different setting

Several of the hardest problems in the two ecosystems are really the same problem in disguise.

Water that plants cannot use

In a boreal winter, roots cannot replace water lost from the leaves because the soil is frozen, so winter is a drought for trees.{{cite:saupe_conifers}} In a salt marsh, water is everywhere but full of salt. Both conifers and cordgrass therefore guard their water: conifers with waxy, narrow needles, cordgrass by filtering salt at the root and secreting it from the leaves.{{cite:saupe_conifers,noaa_est07}}

Soil without oxygen

Black spruce grows on cold, waterlogged peat and permafrost, keeping most of its roots in the top 20 cm of soil.{{cite:feis_picmar}} Smooth cordgrass grows in airless marsh mud and pipes oxygen down to its roots through aerenchyma.{{cite:usda_spal}} In both places, waterlogging is what lets peat build up.

Slow decay, big carbon stores

Cold slows decomposition in the boreal forest, so needles and moss pile up into a thick organic layer; waterlogging slows it in a marsh, so dead plant matter accumulates as peat several feet thick. Both ecosystems store large amounts of carbon in their soils.{{cite:saupe_conifers,clemmensen2013,noaa_est06,mcowen2017}}

Disturbance as a reset button

In the forest, fire opens serotinous lodgepole pine cones and recycles nutrients.{{cite:feis_pinconl,usfs_rxfire}} In the estuary, the tide floods the marsh once or twice a day, delivers food and sets the schedule for fiddler crabs, periwinkles and mummichogs.{{cite:noaa_est01,adw_uca,cbp_mummichog}}

Timing is everything

Snowshoe hares change coat color on a schedule set mainly by day length, which is why a late snowfall can leave white hares on brown ground.{{cite:zimova2018,mills2013}} Fiddler crabs follow an internal tidal clock, and mummichogs lay eggs on the highest tides of the month.{{cite:bennett1957,cbp_mummichog}}

Few species, many individuals

Harsh, changeable conditions filter out most species. The boreal forest has relatively few tree species, and far fewer organisms can tolerate the estuary’s salinity swings than live in stable fresh or salt water.{{cite:ucmp_forests,noaa_est07}}

How the two ecosystems are connected

1

Rivers carry the forest to the sea

Estuaries collect whatever their watersheds send downstream.{{cite:noaa_est03}} Several estuaries on our world map, such as the St. Lawrence and the Columbia, are fed by rivers that drain large areas of coniferous forest (compare the forest map). What happens to forests upstream, from logging to fire, therefore shapes the water that reaches the estuary.

2

Forests protect estuary water quality

In the Chesapeake Bay watershed, forests capture more than 85% of the airborne nitrogen that falls on them, nitrogen that would otherwise feed algae blooms in the bay.{{cite:cbp_facts}} Protecting forests upstream therefore also protects the estuary downstream.

3

Both store carbon, and both can lose it

Boreal soils and marsh peat both hold large carbon stores.{{cite:clemmensen2013,mcowen2017}} Warming threatens both: more frequent boreal fires release carbon from trees and organic soil, while rising seas can drown marshes faster than they can move inland.{{cite:jain2024,cbp_climate}}

4

Climate change at both ends of the river

The Arctic has warmed nearly four times faster than the globe as a whole since 1979.{{cite:rantanen2022}} On the coast, sea-level rise is creating ghost forests where salt water kills coastal trees.{{cite:noaa_ghostforest}} In both places, species are under pressure to move, and protected corridors or room to migrate inland are part of the solution.{{cite:cbp_climate}}

Review: compare and explain

Questions that ask you to connect the two ecosystems. Click a question to reveal a model answer.

Why is the coniferous forest called a biome but the estuary a life zone?

Biomes are land ecosystems classified mainly by climate (temperature and precipitation). Aquatic life zones are classified by water conditions such as salinity, depth and flow; an estuary is defined by the mixing of fresh and salt water, not by its climate.

Compare the main limiting factor in each ecosystem.

In the forest it is cold: a short growing season, frozen soil and slow decomposition that keeps nutrients locked up. In the estuary it is salinity that changes with every tide and season, plus low oxygen in waterlogged mud.{{cite:saupe_conifers,noaa_est07}}

How do black spruce and smooth cordgrass each cope with waterlogged soil?

Black spruce keeps most of its roots in the shallow upper layer of organic soil above cold, wet peat or permafrost. Cordgrass moves oxygen from its leaves to its roots through spongy aerenchyma tissue.{{cite:feis_picmar,usda_spal}}

Which ecosystem is more productive, and why?

The estuary, per area: rivers deliver nutrients from the whole watershed and tides mix them. The boreal forest is limited by cold and nutrient-poor soil, although its huge area makes it globally important for carbon storage.{{cite:noaa_est03,feis_picmar}}

Give one example of a trophic relationship that controls each ecosystem.

Forest: the roughly ten-year snowshoe hare and Canada lynx cycle. Estuary: blue crabs eat marsh periwinkles, which otherwise overgraze cordgrass; removing the crabs can turn marsh into mudflat.{{cite:krebs2001,silliman2002}}

Why can pollution on a farm far inland harm an estuary?

Every stream in the watershed drains to the estuary. Fertilizer and manure washed off fields carry nitrogen and phosphorus that fuel algae blooms, and decomposing algae use up oxygen, creating dead zones.{{cite:epa_ag,cbp_do}}

How is fire in the forest similar to tides in the estuary?

Both are regular natural disturbances that species depend on: lodgepole pine cones open after fire, and many estuary animals time feeding and reproduction to the tides.{{cite:feis_pinconl,adw_uca}}
